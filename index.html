Key takeaway

Keeping a Melbourne kitchen renovation on budget starts with labelling the scope before any price is discussed. A surface refresh covering door fronts, a benchtop and splashback typically sits near $12,000 to $30,000, while a complete rebuild with new cabinetry, benchtops, plumbing and electrical updates commonly lands between $25,000 and $60,000, and premium work with natural stone and custom joinery can climb past $80,000. Most cost blowouts trace back to unclear inclusions, late layout decisions and variations added after signing, all of which a careful brief and written fixed-price quote can prevent.

Kitchen Renovations Melbourne Budget Tips Explained

Most renovators find that the final figure has less to do with tiles and tapware than with four structural choices: layout, joinery, benchtop material, and trade changes. Moving plumbing or electrical points adds licensed trade work. Made-to-measure cabinetry costs more than flat-pack runs but suits period terraces, Edwardian homes and bungalows where the aim is to respect the existing character. Apartment kitchens across Richmond, Brunswick, Footscray and the inner city introduce their own pressures, including owners corporation documentation, compact galley and single-wall layouts, acoustic underlay and clever storage, as covered in this guide.

  • Layout changes are usually the largest single budget driver because they pull in plumbing and electrical trades.
  • Joinery detail such as soft-close runners and hardware brands can be priced as upgrades rather than included.
  • Benchtop selection and splashback specification can shift a quote by thousands between comparable renovators.
  • Appliance dimensions locked in late can delay fabrication and compress the installation window.

Budget Tips That Protect the Final Figure

Treat the published ranges as a screening tool, not a quote. The practical discipline is to set a rough budget, test it against the indicative bands, and only then book an in-home consultation where measurements and finish selections convert into a fixed price.

  1. Define scope in writing first. Note whether the job is a refresh, a full renovation, a custom joinery project or an apartment kitchen before anyone quotes.
  2. Separate wants from load-bearing decisions. Final layout, benchtop choice, hardware selection and appliance dimensions drive fabrication lead times, so settle them early.
  3. Compare exclusions, not just totals. When two quotes sit far apart, the gap is often explained by what one renovator left out rather than a genuine price difference.
  4. Ask how variations work. Understand what happens to the contract price when the brief changes mid-build.
  5. Keep a contingency mindset. The ranges are indicative figures to test realism, and the written fixed-price quote after measurement is the number that matters.

Refresh or Rebuild: What Each Level Buys You

The clearest budget conversation is one that labels scope before price. A refresh updates the visible surfaces without moving the layout. A full renovation replaces cabinetry, benchtops and splashback, updates plumbing and electrical work, and includes soft-close hardware, with design, manufacture, demolition and installation handled under a written fixed-price contract. A larger layout may also model a butler's pantry, which should be specified rather than assumed.

For timing, plan on roughly six to twelve weeks from signing to handover for a mid-range full project, split into about four to six weeks of cabinetry and stone fabrication, two to four weeks on site once cabinetry is ready, and one to two weeks of finishing. The visible demolition and installation stage is only part of the job; the front half is driven by cabinet, stone and appliance lead times.

Questions That Surface Hidden Costs Early

The cheapest-looking quote is often the one with the longest exclusion list. Before signing, ask renovators to identify which decisions could delay fabrication, and confirm in writing what the base price covers.

  • Does the quote include design, manufacture, demolition, installation and finishing, or only some of those stages?
  • Are plumbing and electrical changes included, or will they be treated as variations?
  • Is the hardware brand and soft-close runner quality itemised in the base price?
  • For a larger layout, is a butler's pantry being modelled or simply assumed?
  • Where the project is large enough to require a formal domestic building pathway, how will the written contract, transparent variation process and any required domestic building insurance be handled?

Matching the Budget to the Property Type

Melbourne's housing stock changes what a sensible budget looks like. Period homes often justify made-to-measure joinery over a flat-pack run where character retention matters. Post-war brick homes usually offer more layout flexibility, which can lower joinery complexity. Apartment owners face constraints that reward spending on storage, bench space and access rather than footprint.

General renovation budgeting principles from ASIC Moneysmart support putting quotes in writing and comparing them before committing. Where the job is only an appliance swap, painting or a single trade repair, a full renovator is unlikely to be the right fit; the better question is whether a narrower trade quote covers the work. Readers weighing local renovators can also consult this guide for a structured comparison of approaches.

  1. Write the scope. Record whether the job is a refresh, full renovation, custom joinery project or apartment kitchen before requesting quotes.
  2. Test the budget. Compare the rough budget against the indicative ranges to check the desired finish level is realistic.
  3. Prepare property details. Have the suburb, property type, kitchen size and any owners corporation requirements ready for the renovator callback.
  4. Scrutinise the written quote. Confirm inclusions, exclusions, hardware, trades, fabrication lead time and the variations process before signing.

Common questions

Why do two Melbourne kitchen quotes come in so far apart? Differences usually sit in exclusions rather than the headline rate. Benchtop material, joinery detail, appliance allowances, splashback specification and trade changes all move the result, so compare what each quote leaves out before assuming one renovator is cheaper.

How much should I allow for a full kitchen renovation in Melbourne? Indicative figures place most complete projects somewhere between $25,000 and $60,000 once fitted, with premium work involving natural stone, custom joinery and a butler's pantry able to exceed $80,000. These are screening figures; the written fixed-price quote after measurement is the number to rely on.

What causes kitchen renovation timelines to blow out? Late decisions on final layout, benchtop choice, hardware selection and appliance dimensions can stall fabrication, and fabrication drives the front half of the schedule. Locking design sign-off, site measurements and ordering early keeps the on-site stage on track.
